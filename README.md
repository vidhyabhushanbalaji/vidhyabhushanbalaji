## Some of my projects

### [JYTeams](https://github.com/charles-h-davis/JYTeams)
Mine and Charles Davis submission to DurHack 2026, for Accenture's prompt to use AI to solve a frustrating process. 
We spent time speaking to Accenture's SWEs to understand what the main pain points in day to day work were, and identified large difficulties in identifying the files they need, and waste time in identifying them due to search processes which return thousands of results, when just one file is needed. JYTeams takes a directory of files and stores cached analysis at each directory, to allow faster access to the right information from a dedicated search function, or direct integration into tickets.
While it didn't win any awards, we received strong feedback that "I could see myself using this" from one of the Accenture judges.
Have a look at our repository from the link in the title, or [see our devpost submission here](https://devpost.com/software/JYTeams)

### [CreditMate](https://github.com/charles-h-davis/JYCreditAssistant)
Mine and Charles Davis submission to WHACK 2026 which achieved 3rd place on Capital One's track. 
We wanted to make the effect of specific purchases in day to day life clearer to understand in terms of borrowing and repayment.
Have a look at our repository from the link in the title, or [see our devpost submission here](https://devpost.com/software/creditmate)

### [HelpMeTutor](https://github.com/vidhyabhushanbalaji/tutoring)
I got frustrated with how long it was taking me to keep records of my tutoring client's lessons, payments and scheduling so made this to help.
I learnt a lot through this project on how the full stack engineering process works, including a Node.js backend, React frontend, Supabase DB and Vercel hosting. I'd never used any of these before, and learnt slowly and properly how these all fit together. Have a look at the repo in the link above, writing this (mostly) manually taught me a lot about how these systems work.
